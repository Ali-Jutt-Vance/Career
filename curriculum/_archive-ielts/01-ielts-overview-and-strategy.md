# Phase 10 — Chapter 1: IELTS Overview, Scoring, and Strategy

---

## Chapter Overview

IELTS (International English Language Testing System) is accepted by more than 12,000 organisations worldwide — universities, employers, professional bodies, and immigration authorities. It does not test your intelligence, your technical knowledge, or how "correct" your accent is. It tests four measurable skills against a published rubric.

That last point is the single most important idea in this entire phase: **IELTS is a rubric-driven exam, not a language ability contest.** Two candidates with identical English can score 6.0 and 7.5 because one of them knows what the four criteria reward and the other is guessing. This chapter makes the rubric explicit so every hour you spend afterwards is aimed at something that actually moves your band.

**Topics:**
- IELTS Academic vs. General Training — and which one *your* target actually requires
- Paper-based vs. computer-delivered IELTS, IELTS for UKVI, and One Skill Retake
- The band scale (1–9), what each band means, and how the overall score is calculated
- Raw-score → band conversion tables for Listening and Reading
- What separates Band 6 from 6.5 from 7 from 8 in Writing and Speaking
- Real institutional requirements (UK, Canada, Australia, EU tech employers)
- The diagnostic test protocol — finding out where you actually stand
- Three study plans: 4-week, 8-week, and 12-week
- A repeatable daily 2-hour routine
- Common mistakes that cost half a band, and test-day logistics

## Beginner Theory

### Why IELTS Exists and What It Actually Measures

Universities and immigration departments need one comparable number for applicants from hundreds of different education systems. A degree taught in English in Lahore, Lagos, and Manila cannot be compared directly, so IELTS provides a common scale produced under controlled conditions: same task types, same timing, same marking rubric, trained and re-certified examiners.

Two consequences follow, and both are practical:

1. **The exam is predictable.** The task types, the timings, and the criteria almost never change. Everything you practise transfers directly to test day.
2. **The exam rewards visible, countable features.** An examiner has roughly 10 minutes with your two essays. They are trained to look for specific evidence: a clear position, paragraph structure, range of vocabulary, complex sentence forms used accurately. If a feature is not visible on the page or audible in the room, it does not exist for scoring purposes.

### The Two Versions

```
IELTS Academic
  Purpose:   University admission (undergraduate, postgraduate), professional
             registration (medical/engineering councils), some visa routes
  Listening: Identical to General Training
  Reading:   3 long academic passages from books, journals, newspapers
  Writing:   Task 1 = describe a graph/chart/table/process/map (150+ words)
             Task 2 = academic essay (250+ words)
  Speaking:  Identical to General Training

IELTS General Training
  Purpose:   Work visas, migration to Australia/Canada/NZ/UK, vocational training,
             secondary education
  Listening: Identical to Academic
  Reading:   Section 1 = 2–3 short everyday texts (notices, ads, timetables)
             Section 2 = 2 workplace texts (contracts, staff handbooks)
             Section 3 = 1 longer general-interest text
             Noticeably easier than Academic Reading, but the band conversion
             is stricter to compensate — see the tables below
  Writing:   Task 1 = a letter, formal / semi-formal / informal (150+ words)
             Task 2 = essay, same style as Academic but slightly more accessible topics
  Speaking:  Identical to General Training
```

**Choose the wrong version and your score is unusable.** Check the exact requirement of your target institution before booking. Two rules that catch people out:

- **Canada Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker, CEC, FSTP) requires IELTS General Training.** An Academic result is *not* accepted for Express Entry language points. Academic *is* what Canadian universities want for a study permit. These are different applications with different requirements.
- **UK visa routes requiring a SELT** (Skilled Worker, Student, Spouse) require **IELTS for UKVI** — same test content, but taken at a UKVI-approved centre with additional identity checks. A standard IELTS booking will not satisfy the Home Office. Universities that assess English themselves under their own sponsor licence may accept standard IELTS; the university must confirm this in writing.

### Delivery Formats

| | Paper-based | Computer-delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Listening / Reading / Writing | Pen and paper | On screen, typed |
| Speaking | Face-to-face with examiner | Face-to-face with examiner (same) |
| Listening answer transfer | 10 extra minutes to copy answers to answer sheet | 2 minutes to check; you type directly |
| Reading | You can annotate the passage freely | Highlight + notes tool on screen |
| Writing | Handwriting must be legible; word count estimated | Live word count shown; easy editing |
| Results | ~13 days | ~3–5 days |
| Availability | Fixed dates, usually weekly | Many slots, often several per day |

**Recommendation for software engineers: choose computer-delivered.** You type faster than you write, editing an essay is trivial, the live word count removes an entire category of anxiety, and the results arrive in days. The one adjustment to practise: on screen you cannot scribble arrows over a Reading passage, so drill the on-screen highlight workflow at least twice before test day using the official free computer-delivered practice tests at `ielts.org`.

### One Skill Retake

In a growing number of countries you can retake **one** section (Listening, Reading, Writing, or Speaking) within 60 days of your original computer-delivered test, and receive an updated Test Report Form. This is significant for planning:

```
If your target is "7.0 overall with no band below 6.5" and you score:
  L 8.0  R 7.5  W 6.0  S 7.5   → overall 7.5 but Writing fails the sub-requirement

Old option:   retake all four sections (full fee, full risk — your 8.0 could drop)
Now:          retake Writing only, keep the other three scores

Strategy implication: do NOT under-prepare a weak skill on the assumption
you will retake it, but DO stop panicking about one bad section ruining a
strong overall result. Check availability in your country before you book.
```

### Terminology You Will See Everywhere

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Band | Your score on the 1–9 scale, in 0.5 steps |
| TRF | Test Report Form — the official result document, valid 2 years |
| Raw score | Number of correct answers out of 40 (Listening / Reading only) |
| Band descriptor | The published rubric describing each band for Writing / Speaking |
| CLB | Canadian Language Benchmark — Canada's own scale, converted from IELTS |
| SELT | Secure English Language Test — the UKVI-approved version |
| Cue card | The task card in Speaking Part 2 |
| Distractor | A wrong answer deliberately made attractive in Listening / Reading |

## Intermediate Concepts

### The Band Scale Decoded

The official one-line summaries, with what they mean in practice:

| Band | Official label | What it actually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Expert user | Fully operational command. Errors are so rare they read as slips, not as gaps. |
| 8 | Very good user | Handles complex language well. Occasional unsystematic errors. Misunderstands only in unfamiliar situations. |
| 7 | Good user | Handles complex language and understands detailed reasoning. Some inaccuracies and inappropriate usage remain, but they don't impede communication. |
| 6 | Competent user | Generally effective command despite inaccuracies. Can use fairly complex language, especially in familiar situations. |
| 5 | Modest user | Partial command. Copes with overall meaning in most situations but makes many mistakes. |
| 4 | Limited user | Basic competence limited to familiar situations. Frequent problems with complex language. |
| 3 | Extremely limited | Conveys and understands only general meaning in very familiar situations. |
| 2 | Intermittent user | No real communication is possible except very basic isolated words. |
| 1 | Non-user | Essentially no ability beyond a few isolated words. |
| 0 | Did not attempt | No assessable information. |

**The practical takeaway for a Band 7 target:** Band 7 explicitly *allows* errors. You do not need perfect English. You need to attempt complex language and get most of it right, while keeping communication clear throughout. Candidates who write short, safe, simple sentences with zero errors typically score 6.0 — accuracy without range is capped. Candidates who attempt complex structures and get 70% of them right score 7.0.

### How the Overall Band Is Calculated

```
Overall = average of the four section bands, rounded as follows:

  Average ends in .25  →  round UP to the next half band
  Average ends in .75  →  round UP to the next whole band
  Otherwise            →  round to the NEAREST half band

Worked examples:
  L 7.5  R 7.0  W 6.5  S 7.0  → 28.0 ÷ 4 = 7.0    → Overall 7.0
  L 8.0  R 7.5  W 6.0  S 7.0  → 28.5 ÷ 4 = 7.125  → Overall 7.0  (nearest half)
  L 8.0  R 7.5  W 6.5  S 7.0  → 29.0 ÷ 4 = 7.25   → Overall 7.5  (.25 rounds up)
  L 8.5  R 8.0  W 6.5  S 7.0  → 30.0 ÷ 4 = 7.5    → Overall 7.5
  L 8.0  R 8.0  W 6.5  S 7.5  → 30.0 ÷ 4 = 7.5    → Overall 7.5
  L 7.0  R 7.0  W 6.5  S 6.5  → 27.0 ÷ 4 = 6.75   → Overall 7.0  (.75 rounds up)
```

**Two strategic consequences most candidates miss:**

1. **Half a band in your strongest section is often cheaper than half a band in your weakest.** Moving Listening from 7.5 to 8.0 (a matter of drilling Part 4 note completion) can be far less work than moving Writing from 6.0 to 6.5. If you only need the *overall* number, buy the cheapest half-bands available.
2. **But most requirements have a per-section minimum.** "7.0 overall with no band below 6.5" means the average trick cannot save a 6.0 in Writing. Read your requirement carefully before deciding where to invest your hours.

These tables are approximate and can vary by ±1 mark between test versions, but they are close enough to plan with. Treat them as your scoreboard: after every practice test, convert your raw score and log the band, not the raw number.

**The most useful number on this page:** for Band 7 you need **30/40 in Listening** and **30/40 in Academic Reading** — that is, you can get **ten answers wrong** and still hit Band 7. IELTS Reading is not a comprehension test of the whole passage; it is a targeted retrieval task where you are allowed to miss a quarter of it.

### What Separates the Bands in Writing and Speaking

Writing and Speaking are marked on four criteria, each contributing 25% of that section's band. The band for each criterion is averaged.

```
WRITING criteria
  TA / TR  Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2)
           Did you do exactly what was asked, fully, with a clear position?
  CC       Coherence and Cohesion
           Logical progression, paragraphing, linking, referencing
  LR       Lexical Resource
           Range, precision, collocation, word formation, spelling
  GRA      Grammatical Range and Accuracy
           Sentence variety, complex forms, error density, punctuation

SPEAKING criteria
  FC       Fluency and Coherence
           Speaking at length, flow, logical sequencing, discourse markers
  LR       Lexical Resource
           Range, paraphrase, collocation, idiomatic language
  GRA      Grammatical Range and Accuracy
           Variety of structures, error-free sentence proportion
  P        Pronunciation
           Intelligibility, stress, rhythm, intonation — NOT accent
```

The jump from 6 to 7, paraphrased from the official descriptors, in the terms that matter when you self-mark:

| Criterion | Band 6 reality | Band 7 reality |
|---|---|---|
| Task Response | Position is present but unclear or inconsistent; some ideas underdeveloped or irrelevant | Clear position throughout; main ideas extended and supported; nothing off-topic |
| Coherence | Some organisation; linking words present but mechanical or overused | Clear progression; paragraphs have one central topic; linking is varied and mostly natural |
| Lexical Resource | Adequate vocabulary; noticeable repetition; errors in word choice/form that occasionally confuse | Sufficient range for flexibility and precision; less common items attempted; occasional errors that don't confuse |
| Grammar | Mix of simple and complex forms; errors are frequent and sometimes impede meaning | Variety of complex structures; **most sentences are error-free**; errors don't impede meaning |
| Fluency (Speaking) | Willing to speak at length but with repetition, self-correction, and hesitation | Speaks at length without noticeable effort; some hesitation is content-related, not language-related |

**The Band 7 grammar bar deserves special attention: "frequent error-free sentences."** In practice, examiners are looking at roughly the majority of your sentences being clean. This is why Chapter 5 of this phase is a grammar chapter — for most candidates sitting at 6.5, grammar accuracy is the binding constraint, not vocabulary.

### Real Institutional Requirements

Always verify against the current official page for your specific programme or visa route — these change. As a planning baseline:

| Target | Typical requirement | Version |
|---|---|---|
| UK Russell Group MSc (Computer Science) | 6.5–7.0 overall, no band below 6.0–6.5 | Academic |
| Australian / NZ universities (postgraduate) | 6.5 overall, no band below 6.0 | Academic |
| Canadian universities (Master's) | 6.5–7.0 overall, no band below 6.0 | Academic |
| Canada Express Entry — CLB 9 (max language points) | L 8.0 · R 7.0 · W 7.0 · S 7.0 | **General Training** |
| Canada Express Entry — CLB 8 | L 7.5 · R 6.5 · W 6.5 · S 6.5 | General Training |
| Canada Express Entry — CLB 7 (minimum for FSW) | 6.0 in all four | General Training |
| Australia skilled migration — "Proficient" (10 pts) | 7.0 in all four | Academic or GT |
| Australia skilled migration — "Superior" (20 pts) | 8.0 in all four | Academic or GT |
| UK Skilled Worker visa (B1 requirement) | 4.0 in all four | **IELTS for UKVI** |
| UK Student visa (degree level, B2 floor) | 5.5 in all four minimum; university usually demands more | IELTS for UKVI (Academic) |
| Germany / Netherlands / Nordic tech employers | Rarely required; 6.5+ signals comfort in an English-only team | Either |
| Middle East / Singapore tech roles | 6.5–7.5 where an English certificate is requested at all | Either |

Note the UK Skilled Worker row carefully. The English requirement for that visa is **B1 CEFR, which maps to IELTS 4.0 in each skill** — far lower than most people assume. The 6.5–7.0 figures you see quoted online usually come from *university* entry requirements, not the work visa. If your route is employer sponsorship into the UK, the visa English bar is low; your real reason to target 7.0 is the job market and the interview room, not the Home Office.

### Canada CLB Conversion (General Training)

Because Express Entry points hinge on this table, and because Listening has a higher bar than the other skills at every level:

| CLB | Listening | Reading | Writing | Speaking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 8.5 | 8.0 | 7.5 | 7.5 |
| 9 | 8.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 |
| 8 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 |
| 7 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| 6 | 5.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 | 5.5 |

If you are optimising CRS points, **Listening is where the leverage is**: CLB 9 needs 8.0 in Listening (35/40) but only 7.0 in Reading (30/40 — and GT Reading is the easier paper). Many candidates over-invest in Reading and lose CLB 9 on a 7.5 Listening.

## The Diagnostic Test — Do This First

Do not start "studying IELTS." Start by measuring yourself, because the plan depends on the gap.

```
DIAGNOSTIC PROTOCOL (one sitting, ~3 hours, do it this week)

Materials: Cambridge IELTS 18 or 19, Test 1 (official past papers, correct difficulty)
           Or the free full practice test at ielts.org

Setup:     Phone in another room. Timer visible. No dictionary. No pausing audio.
           Sit at a desk, not on a bed. Write with the same tool you'll use on test day.

Order:     Listening (30 min + 10 transfer)
           Reading   (60 min, strict)
           Writing   (60 min, strict — both tasks)
           Speaking  (record yourself: Part 1 4 min, Part 2 1+2 min, Part 3 4 min)

Marking:   Listening and Reading — mark against the key, convert with the tables above.
           Writing — do NOT self-mark yet. Save it. You will re-mark it after
                     Chapter 3 using the checklist there. Alternatively get it
                     marked by a tutor or a scoring service.
           Speaking — listen back and count: long pauses, repeated words,
                      self-corrections, sentences that are all simple present.

Log this (you will re-run this test protocol in week 3 and week 6):

  Section    Raw    Band    Biggest single loss pattern
  Listening  __/40  ___     e.g. "lost 6 marks in Part 4 note completion"
  Reading    __/40  ___     e.g. "lost 5 marks on Matching Headings"
  Writing     —     ___     e.g. "Task 1 had no overview paragraph"
  Speaking    —     ___     e.g. "Part 2 stopped after 55 seconds"
```

The "biggest single loss pattern" column is the whole point. Scores tell you *where* you are; loss patterns tell you *what to do on Monday morning*. IELTS losses are almost always concentrated: one question type, one missing paragraph, one habit.

### Reading Your Diagnostic

```
If your diagnostic overall is 6.5–7.0  → 4-week plan. You need tactics, not language.
If it is 5.5–6.0                       → 8-week plan. Tactics plus grammar accuracy.
If it is 5.0 or below                  → 12-week plan. Language first, exam second.
                                          Booking a test in 4 weeks would waste the fee.

If one section is 1.5+ bands below the others → that section gets 50% of your hours.
If Writing is your lowest (most common) → Chapters 3 and 5 are your priority.
```

## Study Plans

### The 4-Week Plan (diagnostic 6.5+, target 7.0–7.5)

```
WEEK 1 — Tactics per section
  Mon  Read Chapter 4 (Listening & Reading). Drill Listening Part 4 only ×3.
  Tue  Reading: Matching Headings + T/F/NG drills, untimed, 20 questions each.
  Wed  Read Chapter 3 (Writing). Write one Task 1. Self-check with the checklist.
  Thu  Write one Task 2 (opinion type). 40 min, strict.
  Fri  Read Chapter 2 (Speaking). Record Part 1 + Part 2 ×3 cue cards.
  Sat  Full Listening + Reading paper, timed. Convert to bands. Log loss patterns.
  Sun  Review only. Re-listen to every Listening question you missed with the
       transcript open. Mark where in the audio the answer was.

WEEK 2 — Volume on the two weakest sections
  Daily: 1 Listening section + 1 Reading passage, timed (45 min total)
  Daily: 1 Writing task, alternating Task 1 / Task 2 (20 or 40 min)
  Daily: 15 min Speaking out loud — 3 Part 1 answers + 1 cue card, recorded
  Daily: Chapter 6 vocabulary — one topic bank per day, used in 5 written sentences
  Sat:   Full timed Listening + Reading. Compare loss patterns with week 1.

WEEK 3 — Full test simulation
  Mon  Chapter 5 grammar: articles + tenses. Do the error-correction drills.
  Tue  Chapter 5 grammar: complex sentences. Rewrite an old essay using 4 of them.
  Wed  Full Writing paper, 60 min. Then compare against Chapter 7 model answers.
  Thu  Full Speaking mock with a partner or tutor. Recorded. Marked on 4 criteria.
  Fri  Weak-type clinic: the two question types you keep losing marks on, 30 each.
  Sat  FULL TEST, all four sections, correct order, one sitting.
  Sun  Deep review. Every error categorised: knowledge / tactics / carelessness.

WEEK 4 — Taper and consolidate
  Mon–Wed  One full section per day + one writing task. Review Chapter 7 models.
  Thu      Light: re-read all cheat sheets in this phase. One cue card set.
  Fri      Rest. Documents ready. Route to centre checked. No new material.
  Sat      TEST DAY.
```

### The 8-Week Plan (diagnostic 5.5–6.0, target 7.0)

```
Weeks 1–2  FOUNDATION
  Chapter 5 grammar, one topic per day, with drills. This is non-negotiable —
  at 6.0, grammar accuracy is what is holding your Writing and Speaking down.
  Chapter 6 vocabulary: 2 topic banks per week, actively used, not just read.
  Daily: 20 min listening for pleasure (podcasts, TED) + 1 Reading passage untimed.

Weeks 3–4  TACTICS
  Follow Week 1–2 of the 4-week plan above, but untimed first, then timed.
  Every Writing task gets rewritten once after self-checking. The rewrite is
  where the learning happens.

Weeks 5–6  VOLUME
  6 Listening sections + 6 Reading passages per week, timed.
  4 Writing tasks per week (2× Task 1, 2× Task 2), one marked externally.
  Speaking: 20 min daily out loud. Weekly mock with a partner.
  Re-run the diagnostic test at the end of week 6. Expect +0.5 to +1.0.

Weeks 7–8  SIMULATION AND TAPER
  Two full tests per week under exam conditions (Sat and Wed).
  All other days: targeted work on your remaining loss patterns only.
  Final 3 days: no new material. Cheat sheets and rest.
```

### The 12-Week Plan (diagnostic 5.0 or below, target 6.5–7.0)

```
Weeks 1–4   LANGUAGE, not exam.
  Grammar (Chapter 5) end to end, one section every two days, with drills.
  Vocabulary (Chapter 6) — all 12 topic banks, 15 words a day, spaced repetition.
  Input: 30 min English listening + 30 min English reading daily, for enjoyment.
  Output: write 150 words a day on anything. Speak 10 minutes a day out loud.
  No timed practice tests at all in this block. They only demoralise at this level.

Weeks 5–8   Run the 8-week plan's weeks 1–4 (foundation + tactics).

Weeks 9–12  Run the 8-week plan's weeks 5–8 (volume + simulation).

Reality check at week 8: if you are not at 6.0, move the test date. IELTS fees
are significant and a result below your requirement has zero value.
```

### The Daily 2-Hour Routine (use this on any plan)

```
0:00–0:10  Vocabulary review — yesterday's 10 words, out loud, in sentences
0:10–0:40  One timed section (Listening section OR Reading passage)
0:40–1:00  Mark it and, more importantly, review it with the transcript / passage:
           for every wrong answer write WHY it was wrong, in one sentence
1:00–1:40  Writing — one task, timed, then self-check against the criteria checklist
1:40–2:00  Speaking out loud, recorded — 1 cue card + 3 Part 3 questions

Non-negotiable rules:
  Reviewing errors is worth more than doing new tests. 20 min of review per
  30 min of practice, minimum.
  Speaking practice must be OUT LOUD and RECORDED. Thinking answers silently
  builds nothing — fluency is a motor skill.
  Every wrong Listening answer gets located in the transcript. Every single one.
```

## Common Mistakes That Cost Half a Band

```
LISTENING
  ✗ Still writing answer 5 while answer 6 is being spoken
      → Write in abbreviations. Move on instantly. Never look back.
  ✗ Not reading ahead during the pauses between parts
      → In every pause, read the NEXT set of questions and predict answer types.
  ✗ Ignoring the word limit ("NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER")
      → Three words = zero marks, even if the meaning is perfect.
  ✗ Writing the first number you hear
      → Speakers self-correct constantly. The corrected value is the answer.
  ✗ Spelling errors on transfer (paper-based)
      → Use the 10 minutes to check spelling, singular/plural, and capitals.

READING
  ✗ Reading the whole passage before looking at the questions
      → Costs 5–8 minutes per passage. Skim structure, then work question-first.
  ✗ Using your own knowledge instead of the text
      → As an engineer you may know the topic. The exam only rewards the passage.
  ✗ Answering FALSE when the correct answer is NOT GIVEN
      → FALSE needs an explicit contradiction in the text. No contradiction = NG.
  ✗ Leaving blanks
      → There is no negative marking. Every blank is a guaranteed zero.
  ✗ Spending 25 minutes on Passage 3 and rushing Passage 1
      → All questions are worth 1 mark. Bank easy marks first.

WRITING
  ✗ Task 1 with no overview paragraph
      → Caps Task Achievement at Band 5. It is the single most expensive omission.
  ✗ Under-length (T1 <150, T2 <250)
      → Penalised directly. Learn your own handwriting/typing word density.
  ✗ Memorised template sentences ("In this modern era of globalisation...")
      → Examiners discount memorised language; it is not counted as your own.
  ✗ One giant paragraph, or a new paragraph every two sentences
      → Coherence and Cohesion is 25%. Four to five paragraphs, one idea each.
  ✗ Repeating the question's key noun 8 times
      → Signals limited Lexical Resource. Build a paraphrase before you start.
  ✗ Spending equal time on both tasks
      → Task 2 is worth twice as much. 20 minutes / 40 minutes, always.

SPEAKING
  ✗ Two-word answers in Part 1
      → Every answer: 2–4 sentences. Answer, reason, example.
  ✗ Stopping at 50 seconds in Part 2
      → You must fill 2 minutes. Practise with a timer until it's automatic.
  ✗ Reciting a memorised speech
      → Penalised under Fluency and Coherence. Prepare content, not sentences.
  ✗ "Sorry, my English is not good"
      → Never apologise. It cannot help you and it primes the examiner.
  ✗ Mentally translating from Urdu/Hindi/Arabic
      → Causes long pauses and L1 sentence order. Chapter 5 addresses this.
  ✗ Whispering, or covering your mouth
      → Pronunciation is 25% and it is scored on intelligibility.

ACROSS THE BOARD
  ✗ Doing test after test with no error review
      → Volume without review does not raise bands. It only builds stamina.
  ✗ Practising with unofficial free tests of the wrong difficulty
      → Use Cambridge IELTS books and official material for anything timed.
  ✗ Booking the test before knowing your diagnostic band
      → The fee is significant. Measure first, then book with a realistic runway.
```

## Test Day

```
THE WEEK BEFORE
  Confirm test date, time, venue, and format on your booking confirmation
  Check whether Speaking is the same day or a different day (either is normal)
  Do a dry run of the journey to the centre at the same time of day
  Photocopy / photograph your passport; the ID you booked with MUST be the ID
    you bring, and the name must match exactly

THE NIGHT BEFORE
  Pack: passport (or the exact ID used for booking), booking confirmation,
        transparent water bottle with no label, pencils/eraser (paper test)
  No new material. Re-read the cheat sheets in this phase only.
  Sleep 8 hours. Set two alarms.
  Do not do a full practice test. Fatigue costs more than one more test gains.

ON THE DAY
  Arrive 45 minutes early. Registration involves photo and biometrics and queues.
  Personal items go in a locker. No phone, watch, or notes in the room.
  Eat something with slow carbohydrates. The written sections run ~2h45m
    with no break between Reading and Writing.

DURING — LISTENING
  Read ahead in every pause. Predict the answer TYPE for each gap.
  Write on the question paper as you hear, in shorthand.
  If you miss one, abandon it immediately and protect the next three.
  Paper-based: use the 10 transfer minutes for spelling, plurals, word limits.
  Computer-delivered: use the 2 minutes to check word limits and typos.

DURING — READING
  Passage 1: 16–17 min. Passage 2: 20 min. Passage 3: 23–24 min.
  Do the question types you are strongest at first, within each passage.
  Never exceed 2 minutes on any single question. Mark it, guess, move on.
  At 57 minutes: fill every remaining blank with a plausible guess.

DURING — WRITING
  Task 1 first, 20 minutes, hard stop. If it isn't finished, move on anyway.
  Task 2: 5 min plan (position + 2 main ideas + 1 example each), 30 min write,
    5 min check for articles, plurals, verb agreement, and spelling.
  Count words once, at the end — do not count as you write.

DURING — SPEAKING
  Greet the examiner normally. The recorder starts immediately; small talk is scored.
  Part 1: answer + reason + example, then stop. Don't ramble past 4 sentences.
  Part 2: use all 60 seconds of prep to write 5 keywords, then talk for 2 minutes.
  Part 3: it is a discussion. Opinion, reason, example, and a hedge or a concession.
  If you don't understand: "Sorry, could you rephrase that?" — this is not penalised
    in Part 1 and 3. In Part 2 you cannot ask about the card content.
  If you make an error, correct it once and move on. Do not re-explain.

AFTERWARDS
  Results: ~3–5 days (computer-delivered) or 13 days (paper), online first.
  If one section misses your requirement, check One Skill Retake availability
    within 60 days before booking a full retake.
  Enquiry on Results (remark) is possible within 6 weeks; it is most often
    worthwhile for Writing or Speaking, and only if you were close to a boundary.
```

## Interview Preparation

**Q1: What is the difference between IELTS Academic and General Training, and which should I take?**
A: Both share identical Listening and Speaking papers. Academic has three long academic Reading passages and a Writing Task 1 that requires describing a graph, chart, table, process, or map. General Training has shorter everyday and workplace Reading texts and a Writing Task 1 that requires a letter. Academic is for university admission and professional registration; General Training is for work visas and most migration routes. The version is dictated by your target, not by preference: Canadian Express Entry accepts only General Training for language points, while a Canadian university will require Academic. General Training Reading looks easier but converts more harshly — Band 9 requires all 40 answers correct, and Band 7 requires 34 out of 40 versus 30 out of 40 in Academic. Always confirm the requirement on the institution's own current admissions or visa page before booking, because taking the wrong version means the result cannot be used at all.

**Q2: How is the overall band score calculated, and can a strong section rescue a weak one?**
A: The four section bands are averaged, then rounded: an average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band, .75 rounds up to the next whole band, and anything else rounds to the nearest half band. So L 7.0, R 7.0, W 6.5, S 6.5 averages 6.75 and becomes an overall 7.0. A strong section can therefore lift the overall figure — but most requirements also impose a per-section minimum, typically "no band below 6.5". In that case a 6.0 in Writing fails the requirement regardless of a 7.5 overall. Read the requirement first: if only the overall number matters, buy the cheapest available half-band, which is usually Listening. If per-section minimums apply, the weakest section is the only one that matters and it should get the majority of your study hours.

**Q3: I'm at 6.5 and need 7.0. What is the fastest thing I can change?**
A: For most candidates at 6.5 the binding constraint is grammatical accuracy in Writing, and the fastest measurable gain comes from three things. First, error density: Band 7 requires frequent error-free sentences, so drilling articles, subject–verb agreement, and plurals — the three highest-frequency error categories — removes more penalty than learning new vocabulary. Second, Task 1's overview paragraph: omitting it caps Task Achievement at Band 5 and instantly drags the Writing average down; adding one sentence beginning "Overall," fixes it. Third, Listening Part 4, which is where most candidates lose four to six marks; drilling note completion alone often moves Listening half a band, which can lift the rounded overall. Do those three before touching anything else, and re-measure with a full timed paper rather than assuming.

**Q4: Are online IELTS practice tests good enough, or do I need the Cambridge books?**
A: For untimed skill drills — a Reading passage to practise Matching Headings, a Listening section to practise prediction — free online material is fine. For anything you intend to convert into a band score, use official material only: the Cambridge IELTS series (books 15–20 are closest to current difficulty) and the free official practice tests on ielts.org. Unofficial tests are frequently mis-calibrated, either flattering you with easy passages or crushing you with texts harder than the real exam, and both distort your study plan. Since your plan is driven by measured loss patterns, the measurement instrument has to be trustworthy. Practise widely, but measure only with official papers.

**Q5: How long is IELTS valid, and when should I take it relative to applications?**
A: A Test Report Form is valid for two years from the test date, and most institutions require it to be valid on the date they assess your application, not the date you apply. Take the test early enough that a retake is possible: for computer-delivered tests results arrive in three to five days and One Skill Retake can be booked within sixty days, so a comfortable plan books the first attempt at least three months before the earliest application deadline. Avoid the opposite mistake of testing too early in your preparation on the theory that you can always retake — each attempt carries a full fee, and a result well below your requirement adds nothing to your file.

## Practical Tasks

### Beginner (10 Tasks)
1. Identify your exact target: institution, programme or visa route, overall band, and per-section minimums. Write it at the top of a notebook page.
2. Confirm from the official source whether you need Academic or General Training, and whether IELTS for UKVI is required.
3. Check whether computer-delivered IELTS and One Skill Retake are available in your city.
4. Sit the full diagnostic test described above in one sitting, under exam conditions.
5. Convert your Listening and Reading raw scores to bands using the tables in this chapter.
6. Write your four section bands and your rounded overall score. Compare with your target.
7. Fill in the "biggest single loss pattern" column for all four sections.
8. Choose your plan (4, 8, or 12 weeks) based on the diagnostic reading guide.
9. Put the daily 2-hour routine into your calendar as fixed appointments for the next 14 days.
10. Order or download Cambridge IELTS 18, 19, and 20.

### Intermediate (10 Tasks)
1. Re-mark your diagnostic Listening with the transcript open, locating every missed answer in the audio.
2. Categorise all your Reading errors by question type and count them. Name your worst two types.
3. Build a one-page error log template with columns: date, section, question type, error, cause, fix.
4. Take one timed Listening paper and one timed Reading paper on the same day, then convert both to bands.
5. Time yourself on each Reading passage separately and compare against the 17/20/23 minute targets.
6. Write out the band-6-versus-band-7 table from memory, then check it.
7. Calculate three different section-score combinations that would give you exactly your target overall.
8. Work out which single half-band improvement is cheapest for you, and justify it in writing.
9. Record a full Speaking test on your phone and time each part against the official durations.
10. Book your test date, working backwards from your plan length plus one week of buffer.

### Advanced (10 Tasks)
1. Complete two full four-section tests in one week under strict exam conditions.
2. Get one Writing paper marked by a qualified tutor or an examiner-marked service, and compare their band with your self-assessment.
3. Build a personal loss-pattern dashboard across at least five practice tests and identify the trend per question type.
4. Design and run a one-week "clinic" targeting only your two weakest question types, 30+ questions each.
5. Simulate a bad-day scenario: do a full test after a poor night's sleep, and note which section degrades most.
6. Practise the paper-based transfer step under time pressure and count the transfer errors you make.
7. If computer-delivered, complete two official on-screen practice tests to internalise the highlight and notes tools.
8. Write your own test-day plan minute by minute, including arrival time, and rehearse the journey.
9. Prepare a decision rule in advance: which single section you would retake under One Skill Retake, for each plausible score outcome.
10. Re-run the full diagnostic protocol and confirm at least +0.5 improvement before test day; if not, move the date.
