# Phase 12 — Chapter 1: The 137-Day Plan

> *"A goal without a date is a wish. A date without a daily task is a fantasy."*

---

## Chapter Overview

This is the operating schedule for the whole book: **137 days**, **20 weeks**, **374 scheduled tasks**, **442 hours** of deliberate work, running from **Tuesday 18 August 2026** to **Friday 1 January 2027**.

Of those days, **120 are working days and 17 are rest days**.

### Two budget regimes

The plan runs at two different intensities, switching on **Tuesday 1 September 2026**.

| | Weeks 1–2 (18–31 Aug) | Weeks 3–20 (1 Sep → 1 Jan) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon–Fri | 4h 30m | **3h** |
| Saturday | 7h | **6h** |
| Sunday | 7h | **OFF** |
| Weekly total | 36h 30m | **21h** |

The first two weeks are a deliberate sprint while motivation is highest and the material is foundational. From September the plan settles into **21 hours a week, held for eighteen straight weeks**. That is the number that matters, because a schedule you abandon in October is worth less than a smaller one you keep.

### Sunday is off

Every Sunday from Tuesday 1 September 2026 carries **zero tasks**. This is not slack in the plan; it is load-bearing. Eighteen weeks is long enough that recovery stops being optional, and a plan with no scheduled rest gets abandoned rather than adjusted. The streak counter in the app treats Sundays as neutral — resting does not break it.

The weekly review moved to the last Saturday slot so that Sunday stays genuinely empty.

### What the reduced budget cut

Dropping from 36 to 21 hours a week is a 38% cut, and pretending the same content fits would be dishonest. These were removed:

- **Project 3 (full-stack Next.js app)** — replaced by a one-page portfolio site in week 16
- **Frontend phase** — demoted to reference reading rather than scheduled study
- **GraphQL, MongoDB, TypeORM, EKS, CloudFormation** — reference only
- **LeetCode volume** — from ~250 problems to ~140, weighted toward Mediums
- **Proposal volume** — from 15 a week to 10

These were protected, because they are what decides a senior offer:

- **PostgreSQL depth** — query plans, indexes, transactions. The biggest interview differentiator.
- **System design** — two full weeks plus the named case studies.
- **AI engineering** — the whole RAG pipeline with measured retrieval quality.
- **Authentication** — refresh rotation, OAuth, multi-tenancy.
- **IELTS** — the complete ramp to the exam, untouched.
- **Applications** — still five every weekday from 6 October.

### The four tracks

| Track | What it is | Why it runs continuously |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering** | Chapters, code, two shipped projects | Skill decays without daily contact |
| **IELTS** | Band 7.5+, exam Saturday 14 November 2026 | Language gains come from frequency, not duration |
| **Business** | Proposals, clients, contracts, revenue | A pipeline fed intermittently produces nothing |
| **Job Hunt** | Applications, interviews, offers | Applications take 4–8 weeks to convert |

### The daily budget, from Tuesday 1 September 2026

**Weekday — 3 hours**

| Time | Block | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `05:30–07:00` | Deep Study | 90 min |
| `12:30–13:00` | Outreach | 30 min |
| `21:00–22:00` | Build | 60 min |

**Saturday — 6 hours**

| Time | Block | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `08:00–11:00` | Deep Build | 180 min |
| `11:30–13:00` | Study | 90 min |
| `15:00–16:30` | Drill + Review | 90 min |

**Sunday — rest.**

The dawn block is the reason this works while employed. Ninety minutes before work, five days a week, is thirty-two hours a month that nothing can take from you — no late meeting, no exhausting day, no social obligation. Evening study is the first thing life cancels. Morning study is not.

At three hours a day the evening block is a single hour, which changes how you use it: it is for **building and drilling**, not for reading. Reading happens at dawn when your mind is clear. If you find yourself reading documentation at 21:00, the day has gone wrong.

## How to use this

1. **Open the Plan tab every morning.** It opens on today. Do not plan your day yourself — it is already planned.
2. **Tick tasks as you complete them.** Progress persists locally. The streak counter is the honest measure.
3. **Missing a day is fine; missing two is a pattern.** Do not "catch up" — resume on today.
4. **Take Sunday off properly.** No guilt, no "just an hour". The rest is what makes week 18 possible.
5. **The weekly review is the last Saturday slot.** Thirty minutes, and it is the highest-leverage half hour of the week.
6. **The milestones are pass/fail.** "Mostly did it" is a no. Be strict — the interviewer will be.

## Self Assessment

At the end of every block:

- Did I hit every milestone, or did I redefine one to make it passable?
- Which of the four tracks did I quietly drop, and what did that cost?
- Is my dawn block actually happening, or has it migrated to the evening?
- Am I genuinely resting on Sundays, or leaking work into them and burning out slowly?
- What is my current streak, and what broke the last one?
