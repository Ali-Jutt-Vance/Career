# Phase 12 — Chapter 1: The 350-Day Plan

> *"A goal without a date is a wish. A date without a daily task is a fantasy."*

---

## Chapter Overview

This is the operating schedule for the whole book: **353 days**, **51 weeks**, **655 scheduled tasks**, **706 hours** of deliberate work, running from **Friday 25 September 2026** to **Sunday 12 September 2027**.

Of those days, **302 are working days and 51 are rest days**.

The plan opens with a short **Week 0 lead-in** — Friday 25 September 2026, the Saturday after it, and a Sunday off — for setting up your machine and your routine. Week 1 proper starts on the Monday after, and every week from there runs Monday to Sunday.

You are doing this while employed full-time. That is the constraint the whole schedule is designed around, and it is why the largest block of the day happens before work rather than after it.

### One budget, no regimes

| | Mon–Fri | Saturday | Sunday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours | **2h** | **4h** | **OFF** |

**Fourteen hours a week, held for fifty straight weeks.** There is no ramp and no second regime. One number, which you either hit or you do not, and which you can measure honestly every Saturday.

This is deliberately half the daily load of the first version of this plan, over more than three times the runway. The total is **higher** — 706 hours against 448 — because a budget you keep for fifty weeks beats a heroic one you abandon in week five. The risk in a plan this long is not intensity, it is drift, which is what the weekly milestones are for.

### Sunday is off

Every Sunday carries **zero tasks**. This is not slack in the plan; it is load-bearing. Fourteen hours a week on top of a full-time job, for a year, is a real load, and a schedule with no scheduled rest gets abandoned rather than adjusted. The streak counter in the app treats Sundays as neutral — resting never breaks it.

### The three tracks

| Track | What it is | Why it runs every day |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering** | Chapters, and code you wrote yourself | Skill decays without daily contact, and yours has decayed for three years |
| **Job Hunt** | CV, GitHub, applications, referrals, interviews, offers | Pipelines take four to eight weeks to convert; you cannot buy that time back later |
| **Communication** | English, explaining your work, interview speech | Good candidates lose final rounds on communication as often as on code |

### The daily budget

**Weekday — 2 hours**

| Time | Block | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `05:00–06:30` | Deep Work | 90 min |
| `13:00–13:30` | Job / English | 30 min |

**Saturday — 4 hours**

| Time | Block | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `08:00–10:00` | Deep Build | 120 min |
| `10:30–11:30` | DSA Drill | 60 min |
| `12:00–13:00` | Review + Apply | 60 min |

**Sunday — rest.**

The dawn block is the reason this works while employed. Ninety minutes before work, five days a week, is thirty hours a month that nothing can take from you — no late meeting, no exhausting day, no social obligation. Evening study is the first thing life cancels, which is why this version does not schedule any. Morning study is not.

The dawn block carries **study on some days and building on others** — the day's spec says which. Do not merge them. A morning that starts as reading and drifts into building is a morning where neither happened.

### Applications are a weekly quota

| From | Per week | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Week 5 | **3** | Targeted only. You are testing the CV and learning the process, not filling a pipeline. |
| Week 13 | **5** | The API is live and linkable. The CV finally points at something. |
| Week 23 | **8** | The data layer is real. You can hold a database conversation. |
| Week 34 | **12** | Two services shipped, Nest and Postgres and Redis. You are the candidate the CV describes. |
| Week 43 | **15** | The push. Ops, AWS and AI on top. Everything you have goes out. |

**390 applications in total** across the plan. That is the funnel size that produces two to four offers, and it ramps because a tailored application from week 23 — with two live projects attached — is worth five from week 5. Five a day for eight months is not a funnel; it is spam, and this market is small enough to remember it.

### The DSA ladder

Every Saturday carries a **60-minute DSA drill**, and it is not random practice — it is a ladder that runs the whole fifty weeks, from arrays and hashing in week 1 to timed mixed sets in week 50. **217 problems in total.** You will sit coding tests from week 5, long before the plan's interview block, which is exactly why this runs from day one rather than waiting until the end.

The drill stays **AI-free for all fifty weeks**, including after the unlock, because interviews are. Phase 1 Chapter 13 has the pattern playbook and the full ladder.

| Weeks | Ladder |
|---|---|
| W1–W4 | Arrays & hashing · Hash maps — frequency & lookup |
| W5–W8 | Two pointers · Two pointers — sorted arrays · Sliding window — fixed size · Sliding window — variable size |
| W9–W12 | Strings — parsing & building · Strings — anagrams & palindromes · Prefix sums · Matrix traversal |
| W13–W16 | Stacks — monotonic & matching · Queues & deques · Linked lists — traversal & reversal · Linked lists — cycle & merge |

| W25–W28 | Heaps — top K · Heaps — streaming median · Recursion — the base-case habit · Backtracking — subsets |

### The rule that makes this work

**For the first eighteen weeks, no AI writes code for you.** Not a line, not a snippet, not a "just this once". You may read documentation, and you may ask an AI to *explain* a concept after you have already tried it yourself. From Monday 1 February 2027 it comes back — as a reviewer and a teacher, never as an author, and always under the rule that you must be able to explain any line it touched.

This is not a moral position about AI. It is a targeted correction. Three years of outsourced thinking is the exact hole this plan is digging you out of, and you cannot dig out of it with a shovel that digs for you.

### Core and Later

**137 chapters are CORE** — the ones this plan schedules. Everything else in the book is **LATER**: reference material for when a job actually needs it, and explicitly *not* something you are behind on. Kubernetes, Terraform, microservices, GraphQL, MongoDB and the rest are all real subjects and none of them will get you this job. A chapter that is not in the plan is not homework you are failing to do.

## How to use this

1. **Open the Plan tab every morning.** It opens on today. Do not plan your day yourself — it is already planned.
2. **Tick tasks as you complete them.** Progress persists locally. The streak counter is the honest measure.
3. **Missing a day is fine; missing two is a pattern.** Do not "catch up" — resume on today.
4. **Take Sunday off properly.** No guilt, no "just an hour". The rest is what makes week 50 possible.
5. **The weekly review is the last Saturday slot.** One hour, and it is the highest-leverage hour of the week.
6. **The milestones are pass or fail.** "Mostly did it" is a no. Be as strict as the interviewer will be.
7. **Ignore everything marked LATER.** If it is not in the week you are on, it is not your problem yet.

## Self Assessment

At the end of every block:

- Did I hit every milestone, or did I quietly redefine one to make it passable?
- Which of the three tracks did I drop, and what did that cost me?
- Is my dawn block actually happening, or has it migrated to the evening and then to nowhere?
- Did the AI rule hold, honestly, or did it start writing again when a task got hard?
- Am I genuinely resting on Sundays, or leaking work into them and burning out slowly?
- What is my current streak, and what broke the last one?
