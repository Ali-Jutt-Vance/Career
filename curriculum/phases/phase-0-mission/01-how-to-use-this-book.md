## Chapter Overview

This is not a book you read. It is a system you run for 137 days.

Everything in this folder — 13 phases, 140+ chapters, a dated daily schedule, an interactive reader, and a desktop application — is one artefact with one purpose: **you holding a signed senior engineering offer, or a running client business, on 1 January 2027.**

Most technical books fail their readers in the same way. They are excellent reference material and terrible change agents. You read three chapters in a burst of motivation, bookmark the rest, and six months later nothing about your career has moved. The material was never the problem. The absence of a schedule, a deadline, and a scoreboard was the problem.

So this book has all three built into it.

### What makes this different from a course

| A normal course | This book |
|---|---|
| Teaches a topic | Runs a campaign against a dated goal |
| You decide when to study | Every one of 137 days is already scheduled |
| Success = finishing the content | Success = an offer, a client, and a band score |
| One track (code) | Four tracks in parallel (code, English, business, job hunt) |
| Progress is invisible | Progress is a streak counter and a completion percentage |
| Ends when the videos end | Ends on a specific date: 1 January 2027 |

### The four tracks, and why all four run daily

The single most common failure mode for engineers trying to level up internationally is **sequencing**. They decide to "finish learning first, then apply", or "get the technical skills, then worry about English". Both are wrong, and both cost roughly six months.

Applications take four to eight weeks to convert. If you start applying the day you feel ready, your first offer arrives two months after that — which means "feeling ready" must happen two months before your deadline, not on it. Language ability improves with frequency rather than duration; thirty minutes daily beats a heroic weekend. Client pipelines need constant feeding because most proposals are ignored, and the arithmetic only works at volume.

So the four tracks run simultaneously, every day:

**Engineering** — the chapters, the code, and two shipped, deployed, publicly reachable projects. This is the largest share of the time and the foundation of everything else.

**IELTS** — a Band 7.5+ target, with the exam already anchored to Saturday 14 November 2026. Two or three weekday evenings plus part of Saturday, ramping to a full-intensity week before the exam.

**Business** — proposals, clients, contracts, and revenue. Starting from week 3, three proposals every weekday. This track exists both because you want a business and because paid client work is the fastest way to produce genuine portfolio evidence.

**Job Hunt** — applications, interview preparation, and offers. Opens on 6 October 2026 at five quality applications per weekday, and never stops, not even after you have an offer in hand.

### The structure of the book

**Phase 0 — Mission.** Where you are now, where you are going, the operating rules, and the scoreboard. Read all of it before day one.

**Phases 1–9 — The engineering curriculum.** Programming foundations, backend, databases, frontend, DevOps, cloud, system design, AI engineering, and professional practice. This is the technical substance, taken from first principles to a level you can defend in a senior interview.

**Phase 10 — IELTS and professional English.** Full preparation for Band 7.5+, plus the technical and professional English that matters after the exam is over.

**Phase 11 — Freelancing, clients, and the business.** Platforms, positioning, proposals, pricing, contracts, and the operational systems that turn one-off projects into recurring revenue.

**Phase 12 — The daily execution plan.** All 137 days, dated, with every task assigned to a time slot. This is the chapter you open every morning.

**Phase 13 — Appendix.** Supporting personal documents kept alongside the plan so nothing lives outside the system.

### How the chapters are built

Every technical chapter follows the same arc, because a consistent arc is what allows you to go from beginner to mastery without gaps:

1. **Chapter Overview** — why the technology exists, what problem it solves, who uses it in production, and what the alternatives are. Context first, because unmotivated learning does not stick.
2. **Beginner Theory** — the mental model. What it *is*, explained without jargon.
3. **Basic Examples** — the smallest working code that demonstrates the concept.
4. **Intermediate Concepts** — the real-world usage patterns.
5. **Advanced Concepts** — what separates someone who uses the tool from someone who understands it.
6. **Industry Usage** — how it appears at scale in real companies.
7. **Security** — the attacks and the defences.
8. **Performance** — the cost model and the optimisation levers.
9. **Debugging** — what goes wrong and how you find it.
10. **Interview Preparation** — the questions you will actually be asked, with answers at a senior level.
11. **Practical Tasks · Mini Project · Production Project · Capstone** — graduated building, because reading without building is roughly ten per cent effective.
12. **Self Assessment** — honest checkpoints.
13. **Cheat Sheet** — the reference you return to afterwards.

You will not read every section of every chapter. The daily plan tells you which sections matter on which day. When the plan says *"Ch.7 JWT — signing vs encryption, claims, token lifetimes"*, read those parts and build the thing. Come back for the rest when you need it.

## The reader application

The book compiles into a desktop application. Launch it from the **Career Book** icon on your desktop.

**The Plan tab** opens on today's date automatically. It shows today's tasks in their time slots, tick boxes that persist, your current streak, your completion percentage, and the number of days remaining until 1 January 2027. This is the screen you open at 05:30.

**The Chapters tab** is the full curriculum, searchable across the entire text, with your reading position saved.

**The Interview Q&A tab** collapses the whole book down to just the interview questions, for the fifteen minutes before a call.

Progress is stored locally in the browser profile the app uses. It is yours, it is private, and it survives rebuilds of the book.

## The rules

These are not suggestions. They are the difference between finishing and not.

**1. The dawn block is untouchable.** 05:30 to 07:00, every working day. This exists because evening study is the first thing that life cancels — a late meeting, an exhausting day, a friend visiting. Nothing competes with 05:30. Ninety minutes a day, five days a week, is thirty-two hours a month that cannot be taken from you.

**2. Build every day.** Every chapter produces at least one thing you wrote yourself and ran. Reading a code example is not learning it. If a day passes with no commit, that day did not happen.

**3. Commit publicly every day.** Your contribution graph is read by recruiters and clients. It is the cheapest credibility signal available and it compounds silently for 137 days.

**4. Start applying before you feel ready.** Applications open on 6 October regardless of how prepared you feel. You will feel unready. Everyone does. The funnel takes weeks and you cannot buy that time back later.

**5. Never send the same proposal twice.** Every client proposal references their specific post and their specific problem. Generic proposals have approximately a zero per cent response rate, and sending fifty of them teaches you nothing.

**6. Missing one day is fine. Missing two is a pattern.** Do not try to catch up by doubling — the plan is front-loaded deliberately and cramming breaks the sequence. Resume on today, and record why the gap happened.

**7. Milestones are pass or fail.** Each week ends with a milestone. "Mostly" is a no. Be as strict with yourself as the interviewer will be.

## Practical Tasks

Before day one — 18 August 2026 — do these:

1. Create the GitHub repository `senior-track-2027`, public, with a `LOG.md`.
2. Launch the desktop app and confirm the Plan tab opens on today.
3. Read Phase 0 completely — all six chapters. It takes about an hour and it is the only part of the book you should read straight through.
4. Put the four fixed anchors in your phone calendar with alarms: IELTS booking (22 Aug), applications open (6 Oct), IELTS exam (14 Nov), deadline (1 Jan).
5. Block 05:30–07:00 in your calendar as a recurring, declined-by-default appointment for the next 137 days.
6. Tell one person what you are doing and when it ends. External accountability roughly doubles completion rates.
